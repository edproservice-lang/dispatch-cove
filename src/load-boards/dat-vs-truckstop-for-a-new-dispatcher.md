## The short answer

DAT and Truckstop are the two load boards a new dispatcher hears about first. Both let you search freight, post trucks, and check market conditions. Neither is automatically the only board you need. The useful difference is how each service's rate tools, credit data, and workflow fit the trucks you dispatch, and which paid plan you can justify early on.

## What both boards will do for you

A load board is a marketplace. Carriers and dispatchers search for freight; brokers and shippers post it. DAT, through [DAT One](https://www.dat.com/One), and [Truckstop](https://truckstop.com/carriers/load-board-with-rates/) both offer load search, truck posting, maps of market conditions, and some form of rate or demand data. That shared core is why the “which one is best” question is the wrong first question.

The right first question is: what will you actually do in the first 90 days? If you are [becoming a dispatcher with no experience](/getting-started/how-to-become-a-truck-dispatcher-with-no-experience/), your first board should help you find legal, fitting freight, look up the broker, and decide whether the rate is in the neighborhood of the lane. Features you will not open do not earn their subscription.

Both companies sell several paid tiers. Entry plans emphasize search and posting. Higher plans add tighter rate windows, more saved searches or users, and richer analytics. Published prices and feature lists change, sometimes by equipment type or user count, so treat any number you saw last year as expired until you check the official plan page.

## Rate tools: DAT RateView and Truckstop Rate Insights

This is the comparison experienced dispatchers actually care about.

DAT’s rate product, RateView, is the benchmark many brokers already quote on a call. Higher DAT One plans include lane-rate averages for common equipment types, with shorter look-back windows on the more expensive tiers. The point of the tool is not a single magic number. It is a range and a trend you can take into a negotiation so you are not accepting the first offer on a lane you have never run.

Truckstop’s comparable product is Rate Insights, included on some higher load-board plans, with a separate Rate Insights Advanced offering described on Truckstop’s own product page. It is designed to show a rate, a range, and a trend, and to put paid and posted context next to each other on the richer version. Truckstop also surfaces truck-to-load and market-condition views on the load board itself, which helps you see whether a lane is loose or tight before you call.

The two databases are not identical. They are built from different networks of posts and transactions, so the same lane can show different averages. That is normal. A professional habit is to read the tool you have, then adjust for the real load — appointments, weight, deadhead, and the broker — rather than arguing that one logo is always “higher.”

## Credit, ratings, and factorability

A booked load is only as good as the invoice that follows it. Both ecosystems include tools for looking up the other party before you commit the truck.

Truckstop publishes factorability data on its load-board plans, which is useful when the carrier factors invoices and needs to know whether that broker is typically accepted. Higher Truckstop plans also include broker ratings and a way to report a broker. DAT’s stack includes credit and monitoring tools sold with or alongside the load board; the exact package depends on the plan you buy, so confirm it on the current DAT page rather than assuming every login includes the same credit view.

None of these tools replace your own file. If a carrier’s factoring company has a denied list, that list wins. If you want a cleaner picture of what happens when a broker does not pay, read the difference between [recourse and non-recourse freight factoring](/factoring/freight-factoring-recourse-vs-non-recourse/) before you treat a credit score as a guarantee.

## Workflow and the rest of the office

Search is only the first step. After you find a load, you still have to confirm it, send it to the driver, track the appointment, and invoice it.

Truckstop offers a free TMS alongside some load-board plans, and it also connects to third-party systems. Truxello, for example, is designed so you can search Truckstop from inside the TMS and import a load instead of retyping the rate confirmation.

{% product "truxello" %}

DAT One is built as a single workspace for search, posting, and rate data. If your office already lives in a particular TMS, the useful question is whether that TMS integrates with the board you are buying, not whether the board’s own homepage looks busier.

For a one-truck start, email and a PDF folder can be enough. For a small dispatch office, the board should feed a system that already holds the driver, the documents, and the invoice. Re-entering the same load in three places is how reference numbers go wrong.

## How to choose a plan without overbuying

Start with the equipment you will actually book. A plan recommended for a sprinter or a box truck is not automatically the right plan for dry van or flatbed, and the other way around.

Then decide whether you need rate analytics in month one. If you will only accept broker offers you already understand, a search-focused plan can be enough. If you will negotiate, or if you are learning lanes you have never booked, the rate tool is the feature that usually justifies the higher tier.

Check the official, current prices and included users. A plan billed per user can get expensive once a second dispatcher logs in. Cancel or downgrade something you do not open. A board you barely search is not “coverage.” It is a bill.

If you can afford only one board at the start, pick the one that already holds the brokers and lanes your trucks run. After a few weeks of honest searching, add the second board only if it is producing freight the first one is not.

## A working method for the first months

Set saved searches for the trucks you dispatch — origin radius, equipment, and a pickup window the driver can legally meet. Look at the rate or market tool before you call, then call with a number and a reason, not a hope. Check the broker. Confirm the appointments in writing. Send the rate confirmation to the driver with the things that actually matter: facility names, appointment times, reference numbers, and any driver-assist or lumper notes.

When a lane goes quiet, do not refresh the same search until the truck sits. Widen the origin, consider a short reposition, or post the truck. The board is a tool. The judgment is still yours.


## Common mistakes on a new desk

The first is searching too wide, then calling on freight the truck cannot legally or geographically reach. A load 400 miles from the truck, with a pickup in three hours, is not a lead. It is noise. Tighten the radius until the results match the driver’s remaining hours.

The second is treating the first offered rate as the market. Open the rate tool, look at the range, and decide whether you are being offered the low end, the middle, or something the lane rarely pays. You do not have to win every nickel. You do have to know when you are giving the lane away.

The third is skipping the broker check because the truck is empty. An empty truck is not a reason to haul for a company you cannot invoice with confidence. The fourth is leaving the rate confirmation in your inbox instead of in the driver’s hands, with appointments and reference numbers in writing. The board found the freight. The file is what gets you paid.
