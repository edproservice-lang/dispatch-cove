## The short answer

IFTA quarterly returns, and the tax due with them, are due on the last day of the month after the quarter ends: April 30, July 31, October 31, and January 31 of the following year. If that date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the due date moves to the next business day. Those standard dates come from [IFTA, Inc.’s carrier information page](https://www.iftach.org/Carriers/). Confirm the date that applies to you, including holidays, with your base jurisdiction.

This page is general information, not tax or legal advice. It does not calculate what you owe, and it does not walk through how to file a return.

## The official due-date rule

IFTA, Inc. publishes a quarterly filing calendar for carriers. The standard reporting periods and due dates are:

| Quarter | Reporting period | Standard due date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | January–March | April 30 |
| Q2 | April–June | July 31 |
| Q3 | July–September | October 31 |
| Q4 | October–December | January 31 of the following year |

The same page states that when the due date falls on a weekend or legal holiday, the next business day applies, and that you should confirm the applicable date with your base jurisdiction.

The underlying rule is in the IFTA Articles of Agreement, section R960.100: the tax return and full payment are due on the last day of the month following the close of the reporting period. If that last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the next business day is the due date. The Articles also treat a return filed without full payment as late (R970).

The list of legal holidays is not a single national IFTA calendar. Jurisdictions apply their own holidays. If a date is close to a weekday holiday, ask your base jurisdiction rather than assuming the standard date still holds.

## Due dates for 2026 and 2027

The dates below apply the official weekend rule to the civil calendar. They are not a separate IFTA publication. Check your base jurisdiction for holidays or special relief before you treat a Monday as your last day.

| Quarter | Miles and fuel from | Last day of the following month | Due date after the weekend rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1Q 2026 | January–March 2026 | Thursday, April 30, 2026 | April 30, 2026 |
| 2Q 2026 | April–June 2026 | Friday, July 31, 2026 | July 31, 2026 |
| 3Q 2026 | July–September 2026 | Saturday, October 31, 2026 | Monday, November 2, 2026 |
| 4Q 2026 | October–December 2026 | Sunday, January 31, 2027 | Monday, February 1, 2027 |
| 1Q 2027 | January–March 2027 | Friday, April 30, 2027 | April 30, 2027 |
| 2Q 2027 | April–June 2027 | Saturday, July 31, 2027 | Monday, August 2, 2027 |
| 3Q 2027 | July–September 2027 | Sunday, October 31, 2027 | Monday, November 1, 2027 |
| 4Q 2027 | October–December 2027 | Monday, January 31, 2028 | January 31, 2028 |

You may file earlier. The weekend rule only moves the last day, and only when that last day is a weekend or a legal holiday.

## A return is still due

IFTA, Inc. tells carriers to file with the base jurisdiction and pay any tax due, and that a quarterly return is generally required even if you did not operate during that quarter. The Articles of Agreement (R930.100) require a tax return even if no operations were conducted or no taxable fuel was used during the period.

Your base jurisdiction is also the office that issues the license and decals, sends the return, and can audit records on behalf of the member jurisdictions. Keep the trip and fuel records that support the return. IFTA, Inc. tells carriers to retain supporting records for at least four years from the return due date or the filing date, whichever is later, and to confirm the complete requirement with the base jurisdiction.

How to complete a quarterly return, and the related federal filings such as Form 2290 and UCR, will be covered in later guides on this shelf. This page stops at the calendar.

## IFTA is not IRP

IFTA covers motor fuel use tax reporting and uses a license and decals. The International Registration Plan covers apportioned vehicle registration and uses plates and cab cards. IFTA, Inc. notes that an operation may need both, and that you should ask your base jurisdiction about the requirements that apply to you. Do not treat an IFTA due date as an IRP due date.
